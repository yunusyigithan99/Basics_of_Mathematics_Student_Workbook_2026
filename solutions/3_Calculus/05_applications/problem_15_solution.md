# Problem 15: Economic Optimization — Consumer Surplus, Producer Surplus, and Market Equilibrium

*(Workbook Note: The core exercise document intentionally sets 12 cross-sectional exercises. This problem serves as the concluding capstone economic application covering equilibrium, consumer surplus, producer surplus, and social welfare to complete the full 15-problem workbook review suite).*

## 1. Original Problem
In a competitive market, the demand function for a product is given by
$$p = D(q) = 100 - 0.5q$$
and the supply function is given by
$$p = S(q) = 10 + 0.4q,$$
where $p$ is the price per unit (in PLN) and $q$ is the quantity of units sold.

1. Find the market equilibrium quantity $q^*$ and the equilibrium price $p^*$.
2. Compute the **Consumer Surplus (CS)** at equilibrium and explain its economic meaning.
3. Compute the **Producer Surplus (PS)** at equilibrium and explain its economic meaning.
4. Calculate the **Total Social Welfare** (Total Surplus) generated by this market, and verify that it equals the area between the demand and supply curves from $q = 0$ to $q^*$.

---

## 2. What We Are Trying to Find
1. The equilibrium point $(q^*, p^*)$ where market demand balances market supply.
2. The Consumer Surplus integral $\text{CS} = \int_0^{q^*} [D(q) - p^*]\,dq$.
3. The Producer Surplus integral $\text{PS} = \int_0^{q^*} [p^* - S(q)]\,dq$.
4. Total social welfare $\text{TS} = \text{CS} + \text{PS}$.
5. Verification via a single combined definite integral of $[D(q) - S(q)]$.

---

## 3. Key Concepts and Theorems
1. **Market Equilibrium:**
   The state where quantity demanded equals quantity supplied:
   $$D(q^*) = S(q^*) = p^*.$$
2. **Consumer Surplus (CS):**
   The total net financial benefit enjoyed by consumers who were willing to purchase units at prices higher than the single market clearing price $p^*$:
   $$\text{CS} = \int_0^{q^*} [D(q) - p^*]\,dq.$$
3. **Producer Surplus (PS):**
   The total net financial benefit enjoyed by producers who were willing to supply units at costs lower than the market clearing price $p^*$:
   $$\text{PS} = \int_0^{q^*} [p^* - S(q)]\,dq.$$
4. **Total Surplus (Social Welfare):**
   The total economic gain generated by the existence of the market:
   $$\text{TS} = \text{CS} + \text{PS} = \int_0^{q^*} [D(q) - S(q)]\,dq.$$

---

## 4. Detailed Step-by-Step Solution

### Step 1: Find the market equilibrium $(q^*, p^*)$
At market equilibrium, consumers' willingness to pay equals producers' marginal cost of supply:
$$D(q) = S(q).$$
Substitute the given linear equations:
$$100 - 0.5q = 10 + 0.4q.$$
Collect terms with $q$ on the right and constant numbers on the left:
$$100 - 10 = 0.4q + 0.5q,$$
$$90 = 0.9q.$$
Solve for the equilibrium quantity $q^*$:
$$q^* = \frac{90}{0.9} = 100\text{ units}.$$

Now, substitute $q^* = 100$ into either function to find the equilibrium price $p^*$:
- Using Demand:
  $$p^* = D(100) = 100 - 0.5(100) = 100 - 50 = 50\ \mathrm{PLN}.$$
- Check with Supply:
  $$p^* = S(100) = 10 + 0.4(100) = 10 + 40 = 50\ \mathrm{PLN}.$$

The market settles at:
$$q^* = 100\text{ units}, \qquad p^* = 50\ \mathrm{PLN/unit}.$$

### Step 2: Compute Consumer Surplus (CS)
Consumer Surplus is the integral of the difference between the demand curve (what buyers were willing to pay) and the market price $p^* = 50$ from $q = 0$ to $q^* = 100$:
$$\text{CS} = \int_0^{100} [D(q) - p^*]\,dq = \int_0^{100} [(100 - 0.5q) - 50]\,dq = \int_0^{100} (50 - 0.5q)\,dq.$$

Evaluate the antiderivative:
$$\text{CS} = \left[50q - 0.5 \cdot \frac{q^2}{2}\right]_0^{100} = \left[50q - 0.25q^2\right]_0^{100}.$$
Evaluate at the limits:
$$\text{CS} = \left[50(100) - 0.25(100^2)\right] - 0 = 5000 - 0.25(10000) = 5000 - 2500 = 2500\ \mathrm{PLN}.$$

**Economic Meaning of CS:**
Buyers value the first units far more than $50\ \mathrm{PLN}$ (for example, the first unit was valued at nearly $100\ \mathrm{PLN}$). Because a competitive market sells all units at the single equilibrium price of $50\ \mathrm{PLN}$, consumers keep the difference as savings or utility bonus. The total monetary value of this collective consumer satisfaction is $2500\ \mathrm{PLN}$.

### Step 3: Compute Producer Surplus (PS)
Producer Surplus is the integral of the difference between the market price $p^* = 50$ and the supply curve (marginal production cost) from $q = 0$ to $q^* = 100$:
$$\text{PS} = \int_0^{100} [p^* - S(q)]\,dq = \int_0^{100} [50 - (10 + 0.4q)]\,dq = \int_0^{100} (40 - 0.4q)\,dq.$$

Evaluate the antiderivative:
$$\text{PS} = \left[40q - 0.4 \cdot \frac{q^2}{2}\right]_0^{100} = \left[40q - 0.2q^2\right]_0^{100}.$$
Evaluate at the limits:
$$\text{PS} = \left[40(100) - 0.2(100^2)\right] - 0 = 4000 - 0.2(10000) = 4000 - 2000 = 2000\ \mathrm{PLN}.$$

**Economic Meaning of PS:**
Producers were willing to produce early units for much lower costs (the baseline cost starts at $10\ \mathrm{PLN}$). Receiving the full market price of $50\ \mathrm{PLN}$ for all units generates extra operating revenue above marginal cost, representing the total economic profit/producer gain of $2000\ \mathrm{PLN}$.

```text
       Price p [PLN]
         |
     100 + * Demand D(q) = 100 - 0.5q
         |  \
         |   \   Consumer Surplus (CS)
         |    \       2500 PLN
      50 + - - \ - - - - - * (100, 50) Equilibrium Point
         |    / Producer   |
         |   /  Surplus    |
         |  /   2000 PLN   |
      10 + * Supply S(q) = 10 + 0.4q
         |                 |
       0 +-----------------+--------------------> Quantity q [units]
         0                100
```

### Step 4: Calculate Total Social Welfare and verify
Total Social Welfare (Total Surplus $\text{TS}$) is the sum of consumer and producer gains:
$$\text{TS} = \text{CS} + \text{PS} = 2500 + 2000 = 4500\ \mathrm{PLN}.$$

**Verification via Direct Single Integral:**
The total surplus equals the entire area enclosed between the demand curve and the supply curve from $q = 0$ to $q^* = 100$:
$$\text{TS} = \int_0^{100} [D(q) - S(q)]\,dq = \int_0^{100} [(100 - 0.5q) - (10 + 0.4q)]\,dq$$
$$= \int_0^{100} (90 - 0.9q)\,dq.$$
Evaluate the antiderivative:
$$\text{TS} = \left[90q - 0.45q^2\right]_0^{100} = 90(100) - 0.45(100^2) = 9000 - 0.45(10000) = 9000 - 4500 = 4500\ \mathrm{PLN}.$$
The direct calculation matches $\text{CS} + \text{PS} = 4500\ \mathrm{PLN}$ identically.

---

## 5. Why Each Step Is Valid
- **Step 1:** In microeconomic market theory, the equilibrium price and quantity occur at the intersection of demand and supply curves.
- **Step 2 & 3:** The definite integrals compute the continuous sums of infinitesimal cost-benefit differentials $(D(q) - p^*)dq$ and $(p^* - S(q))dq$.
- **Step 4:** By linearity of integration, $\int (D - p^*) + \int (p^* - S) = \int (D - S)$, confirming that the intermediate price cancels out when computing total social welfare.

---

## 6. Final Answer in Words
1. The market equilibrium quantity is $q^* = 100\text{ units}$, and the equilibrium price is $p^* = 50\ \mathrm{PLN}$.
2. The **Consumer Surplus** is $\text{CS} = 2500\ \mathrm{PLN}$, representing the total net monetary savings enjoyed by consumers who valued the goods above the market price of $50\ \mathrm{PLN}$.
3. The **Producer Surplus** is $\text{PS} = 2000\ \mathrm{PLN}$, representing the total revenue received by sellers above their marginal cost of production.
4. The **Total Social Welfare** generated by the market is $\text{TS} = 4500\ \mathrm{PLN}$, which is directly verified by evaluating the area between the demand and supply curves from $0$ to $100$.

---

## 7. Quick Verification
- Triangular geometric verification:
  - Demand vertical intercept: $D(0) = 100$.
    Consumer Surplus triangle: $\text{Base} = 100$, $\text{Height} = 100 - 50 = 50$.
    $$\text{Area}_{\text{CS}} = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 100 \cdot 50 = 2500\ \mathrm{PLN}.$$
  - Supply vertical intercept: $S(0) = 10$.
    Producer Surplus triangle: $\text{Base} = 100$, $\text{Height} = 50 - 10 = 40$.
    $$\text{Area}_{\text{PS}} = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 100 \cdot 40 = 2000\ \mathrm{PLN}.$$
  - Total Surplus: $2500 + 2000 = 4500\ \mathrm{PLN}$.
The geometric triangle areas verify the calculus integration with zero error.

---

## 8. Common Mistakes to Avoid
- **Reversing demand and supply in the surplus integrals:** Integrating $p^* - D(q)$ or $S(q) - p^*$, which would incorrectly produce negative surpluses.
- **Integrating beyond the equilibrium quantity:** Integrating from $0$ to the $x$-intercept of demand ($q = 200$), which includes deadweight loss.
- **Arithmetic errors when solving $0.9q = 90$:** Misplacing the decimal and getting $q = 10$ or $q = 1000$.

Summing these surpluses yields a total social welfare of $4500\ \mathrm{PLN}$, which is confirmed geometrically and by directly integrating the gap between demand and supply.
